Ingredient Safety Learning Center

Pet food ingredients, contaminants, nutrition and recalls.

Learn what pet-food labels reveal, what contaminants they cannot show and when to check official recalls.

What pet-food labels can tell you

Pet-food labels identify declared ingredients and provide feeding, product and nutrition information. Ingredients may include meat, poultry, grains, fats, vitamins, minerals, flavors, preservatives and processing aids. The label is an important starting point, but it is not a complete laboratory report.

Ingredient familiarity does not prove quality

A familiar-sounding ingredient is not automatically better, and a technical nutrient name is not automatically harmful. Vitamins, minerals and preservatives can serve important purposes. A pet’s species, age, life stage, medical history and total nutrient intake matter more than marketing language alone.

Look beyond the first ingredient

Focusing only on the first ingredient can ignore the overall formula. The full ingredient list, nutrient information, feeding directions and suitability for the individual animal all deserve attention.

Food allergies and intolerances in pets

Pets can react to food ingredients, but symptoms such as itching, vomiting or diarrhea can have many causes. An ingredient scanner cannot diagnose a food allergy. A veterinarian may recommend a controlled elimination diet or other evaluation based on the animal’s history.

Why product identity matters

Brand, product name, package size, lot code and best-by date become especially important during a recall. Keep the original packaging when a pet becomes ill so the exact product can be identified and reported.

Contaminants may not appear as ingredients

Animal food can be affected by harmful bacteria, mycotoxins, pesticides, dioxins, trace elements or foreign material. These may be unintended contaminants rather than ingredients added to the formula, so the ingredient list may not disclose them.

Raw pet-food risks

FDA testing has found raw pet food more likely than other tested pet-food types to contain disease-causing bacteria. Contaminated food can affect pets and may expose people through hands, bowls, utensils and household surfaces.

Mycotoxins and chemical contaminants

Mycotoxins are produced by certain molds and can contaminate agricultural ingredients. FDA also monitors pesticides, industrial chemicals and trace elements in animal food. Confirming contamination requires proper sampling and laboratory testing.

Check official recalls

A barcode may help identify a product, but recalls are often limited to particular lot codes, manufacturing dates or package sizes. Compare the complete package information with official FDA recall notices.

What HealthLogic can and cannot do

HealthLogic can explain declared ingredients and organize available product information. It cannot test the food, diagnose illness or guarantee that an individual package is free from contamination. Contact a veterinarian promptly when a pet has concerning symptoms.
