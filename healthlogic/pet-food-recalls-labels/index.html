Pet Product Safety

Pet food recalls and labels: ingredients, contaminants and safety.

Learn what pet-food labels can show, what they cannot show and how to respond to official recall alerts.

Download on the App Store Get it on Google Play

Scan to download on Android

Keep package and lot information

When buying pet food, keep or photograph the brand, product name, lot code and best-by date. This information can be important if a recall is announced or if a pet becomes ill. A barcode may identify a product, but a lot code often connects a package to a recall.

Labels are not contamination reports

Pet-food labels list ingredients and feeding information, but they do not function as laboratory reports. Contaminants, bacteria and manufacturing issues may require official testing and recall action. Check manufacturer and government recall resources for current information.

Watch your pet, not only the label

Changes in appetite, vomiting, diarrhea or unusual behavior should be discussed with a veterinarian. HealthLogic can clarify publicly available label information, but it does not provide veterinary diagnosis, verify a lot or replace professional care.

Primary source: FDA: Animal Food Recalls, Withdrawals and Alerts.
